Bio

I'm a researcher, policy analyst, and human rights practitioner working at the intersection of migration, peace and conflict resolution, and diaspora identity. I hold an M.A. in International Relations and a B.A. in Political Science from American University.

I currently serve as a Project Coordinator at the Stimson Center, where I support the T20 U.S. team in designing and convening the T20 Summit, its pre-summit dialogue during UNGA high-level week, and related global hybrid events, while facilitating relationships with G20 Sherpas, engagement groups, think tanks, and international organizations, and producing the talking points, briefings, and communications that keep T20 U.S. partners, including the Brookings Institution and the Quincy Institute, coordinated throughout. I was the Director of Women, Peace and Security (WPS) at American University, where I built a community of students across the DMV committed to advancing the WPS agenda.

My research spans continents and methodologies. Most recently, I conducted independent field research in Guyana with Indo-Guyanese, Afro-Guyanese, and Indigenous communities, exploring how cultural practices are preserved across generations in the face of displacement. I contributed to the Roti Collective Lab, using ethnography and ArcGIS to trace how culinary traditions carry migration stories, and to the Mass Atrocity Research Initiative (MARI), where I studied early warning indicators and social resilience.

Before graduate school, I spent nearly three years as an Immigration Analyst at VisaNation Law Group, reviewing family- and employment-based petitions and helping design client-facing tools to improve access to legal services. My earlier experience includes the National Immigrant Women's Advocacy Project, the Suffolk County DA's Office, and the U.S. Congress.
